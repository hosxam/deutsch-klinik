# FSP Target Curriculum

## Overview
The FSP (Fachsprachprüfung) medical German track is a structured learning path for doctors preparing for the German medical language exam. It occupies a space after or alongside B2/C1 German and focuses on clinical communication skills.

## Track Structure
- **Level identifier:** "FSP"
- **Total modules:** 20
- **Total lessons:** 40 (2 per module)
- **Prerequisites:** B2 German recommended, C1 for complex modules (14-20)
- **Estimated total time:** ~3000 minutes

## FSP Modules and Lesson Order

### Module 1: FSP Orientation and Exam Structure (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 1:** Understanding the FSP exam format
- **Lesson 2:** Exam strategies and common pitfalls
- **Vocabulary themes:** Exam structure, evaluation criteria, time management
- **Speaking tasks:** Self-introduction, expectations
- **Writing tasks:** None (orientation)
- **Prerequisites:** None (introductory)

### Module 2: Opening Consultation and Patient Rapport (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 3:** Greeting, introduction, establishing rapport
- **Lesson 4:** Setting the agenda and managing patient expectations
- **Vocabulary themes:** Greetings, introductions, opening phrases
- **Speaking tasks:** Greeting and opening the consultation
- **Writing tasks:** None
- **Prerequisites:** None (basic communication)

### Module 3: Current Complaint / Leitsymptom (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 5:** Eliciting the chief complaint (SOCRATES framework)
- **Lesson 6:** Clarifying symptoms and duration
- **Vocabulary themes:** Pain description, symptom vocabulary, temporal phrases
- **Speaking tasks:** Structured symptom inquiry
- **Writing tasks:** Documenting chief complaint
- **Prerequisites:** Modules 1-2

### Module 4: Pain History (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 7:** Pain characteristics (location, quality, severity, radiation)
- **Lesson 8:** Pain triggers, relieving factors, and progression
- **Vocabulary themes:** Pain adjectives, body regions, intensity scales
- **Speaking tasks:** Comprehensive pain history
- **Writing tasks:** Pain documentation
- **Prerequisites:** Module 3

### Module 5: Past Medical History (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 9:** Previous illnesses, surgeries, hospitalizations
- **Lesson 10:** Chronic conditions and ongoing treatments
- **Vocabulary themes:** Chronic disease vocabulary, surgical history
- **Speaking tasks:** PMH inquiry
- **Writing tasks:** PMH section of Arztbrief
- **Prerequisites:** Modules 1-2, B2 German

### Module 6: Medication and Allergies (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 11:** Current medications (name, dose, frequency, indication)
- **Lesson 12:** Allergies (drug, food, latex) and adverse reactions
- **Vocabulary themes:** Medication forms, dosing, allergy types
- **Speaking tasks:** Medication reconciliation
- **Writing tasks:** Medication list documentation
- **Prerequisites:** Module 5

### Module 7: Family and Social History (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 13:** Family history (genetic risk, family diseases)
- **Lesson 14:** Social history (occupation, lifestyle, smoking, alcohol)
- **Vocabulary themes:** Family relationships, lifestyle vocabulary, occupation
- **Speaking tasks:** Social history inquiry
- **Writing tasks:** Social history documentation
- **Prerequisites:** Modules 1-2

### Module 8: Review of Systems (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 15:** Systematic symptom inquiry per body system

### Module 9: Physical Examination Language (2 lessons)

- **Speaking tasks:** Instruction during examination, reporting findings

### Module 10: Diagnostics and Investigations (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 19:** Lab results and imaging: explaining to patients
- **Lesson 20:** Ordering and interpreting investigations
- **Vocabulary themes:** Lab values, imaging types, diagnostic procedures
- **Speaking tasks:** Explaining test results in patient-friendly language
- **Writing tasks:** Lab result section of Arztbrief
- **Prerequisites:** Modules 8-9

### Module 11: Explaining Diagnoses (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 21:** Framing a diagnosis in patient-friendly German
- **Lesson 22:** Differential diagnoses and uncertainty communication
- **Vocabulary themes:** Diagnosis terminology, lay equivalents, uncertainty phrases
- **Speaking tasks:** Diagnosis explanation
- **Writing tasks:** Diagnosis section of Arztbrief
- **Prerequisites:** Module 10, C1 German

### Module 12: Treatment Plan and Recommendations (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 23:** Explaining treatment options and rationale
- **Lesson 24:** Lifestyle recommendations and preventive care
- **Vocabulary themes:** Treatment vocabulary, lifestyle advice, preventive medicine
- **Speaking tasks:** Treatment plan explanation
- **Writing tasks:** Treatment section of Arztbrief
- **Prerequisites:** Module 11

### Module 13: Informed Consent and Risk Communication (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 25:** Explaining procedures and obtaining consent
- **Lesson 26:** Risk/benefit communication and shared decision-making
- **Vocabulary themes:** Consent vocabulary, risk phrases, probability language
- **Speaking tasks:** Consent discussion
- **Writing tasks:** Consent documentation
- **Prerequisites:** Modules 11-12, C1 German

### Module 14: Doctor-to-Doctor Handover (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 27:** Structured handover (ISBAR protocol)
- **Lesson 28:** Oral case presentation
- **Vocabulary themes:** Handover structure, urgency language, ISBAR framework
- **Speaking tasks:** ISBAR handover, case presentation

### Module 15: Arztbrief Structure (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 29:** Admission note structure and content

- **Vocabulary themes:** Formal medical documentation, abbreviations
- **Speaking tasks:** None (writing focus)
- **Writing tasks:** Full admission note, discharge summary

### Module 16: Common Internal Medicine Cases (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 31:** Cardiovascular and pulmonary cases

- **Speaking tasks:** Full patient encounter (IM focus)

- **Prerequisites:** Modules 1-15

### Module 17: Surgical and Orthopedic Cases (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 33:** Preoperative and post-surgical communication

- **Vocabulary themes:** Surgical vocabulary, orthopedic terminology, rehabilitation
- **Speaking tasks:** Full patient encounter (surgical focus)
- **Writing tasks:** Surgical case documentation
- **Prerequisites:** Modules 1-15

### Module 18: Emergency and Red Flag Communication (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 35:** Recognizing and communicating urgency
- **Lesson 36:** Breaking bad news
- **Vocabulary themes:** Emergency vocabulary, urgency escalation, breaking bad news phrases
- **Speaking tasks:** Emergency communication, bad news delivery (SPIKES protocol)
- **Writing tasks:** Emergency documentation, critical incident reporting
- **Prerequisites:** Modules 10-14

### Module 19: Pediatric, Geriatric, Psychiatric Contexts (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 37:** Pediatric communication (parent-child)
- **Lesson 38:** Geriatric and psychiatric considerations
- **Vocabulary themes:** Pediatric vocabulary, geriatric assessment, psychiatric terminology
- **Speaking tasks:** Age-appropriate communication
- **Writing tasks:** Special population documentation
- **Prerequisites:** Modules 1-14

### Module 20: Full FSP Simulation (2 lessons)
- **Lesson 39:** Complete FSP mock exam (internal medicine)
- **Lesson 40:** Complete FSP mock exam (surgical/emergency)
- **Vocabulary themes:** All modules
- **Speaking tasks:** Full 3-part FSP exam
- **Writing tasks:** Full Arztbrief under time pressure
- **Prerequisites:** All modules 1-19

## Integration with A1-C1 Levels

The FSP track uses existing A1-C1 German grammar and vocabulary as foundation. All FSP-specific content is labeled with level "FSP" in curriculumMap and data files.

### How FSP connects to existing curriculum:
- **Grammatical structures** taught in B2/C1 are reused in FSP context (passive voice, Konjunktiv I, nominalization)
- **Vocabulary** has its own FSP-level entries but references B2/C1 level where appropriate
- **Reading** materials use B2/C1 sentence structures with medical terminology
- **Speaking** tasks assume B2+ speaking ability with medical vocabulary overlay
- **Prerequisites** reference B2/C1 curriculumMap entries where a concept is needed
- FSP does NOT replace any A1-C1 content

## Case Types
1. **Cardiovascular:** ACS, hypertension, heart failure, arrhythmia, peripheral artery disease
2. **Pulmonary:** COPD, asthma, pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, lung cancer
3. **Gastrointestinal:** GERD, peptic ulcer, cholecystitis, pancreatitis, IBD
4. **Endocrine:** Diabetes mellitus, thyroid disorders, metabolic syndrome
5. **Renal/Urology:** Kidney stones, UTI, chronic kidney disease, BPH
6. **Neurology:** Stroke, seizure, headache disorders, neuropathy
7. **Musculoskeletal:** Fracture, arthritis, back pain, joint replacement
8. **Psychiatry:** Depression, anxiety, dementia, substance abuse
9. **Infectious disease:** Sepsis, cellulitis, nosocomial infection
10. **Emergency:** Anaphylaxis, cardiac arrest, trauma, acute abdomen
11. **Surgical:** Preoperative assessment, postoperative complications
12. **Oncology:** New diagnosis, treatment discussion, palliative care

## Progression Logic
1. **Weeks 1-2 (Modules 1-4):** Basic consultation skills (greeting, complaint, pain history)
2. **Weeks 3-5 (Modules 5-9):** Structured history taking (PMH, medications, social, ROS, exam)
3. **Weeks 6-8 (Modules 10-13):** Diagnostic reasoning and treatment (results, diagnoses, plans, consent)
4. **Weeks 9-11 (Modules 14-15):** Documentation and handover (ISBAR, Arztbrief)
5. **Weeks 12-14 (Modules 16-18):** Case-based application (specialties, emergencies)
6. **Weeks 15-16 (Modules 19-20):** Special contexts and full simulation

Each module builds on the previous. Modules 16-20 are integrative capstone modules.
